How the Builder works

The short version: it's a factory with one button. Here's the longer one.

The problem nobody warns you about

Every iPhone app in the world has to be built — compiled, packaged, and signed — by a program called Xcode. And Xcode only runs on a Mac. So if you make apps the way we do — dreaming them up and building them with an AI partner, no code written by hand — you still hit a wall shaped like a computer you don't own.

The usual answers are renting time on someone else's Mac by the minute, or standing in a queue of free build minutes that runs out at the worst moment. We wanted a third answer: own the factory.

The idea

The Forge iPhone App Builder is an app whose whole job is building other apps. Your projects live on it as simple cards. Behind the button sits a "middleman" — a small, private server side that holds your build key safely and talks to a cloud Mac on your behalf. You never see the machinery. You see a button, five friendly steps, and a checkmark.

What happens when you tap Build

  1. Waking up the workshopThe Builder calls its middleman, which securely fetches your build key — stored on the server only, never in the page.
  2. Handing over the plansYour app's project is sent to a cloud Mac provided by Codemagic, a build service — the Mac does the heavy lifting, invisibly.
  3. Building the appThe Mac compiles your app with Apple's official tools and the iPhone SDK, exactly as a Mac on a desk would.
  4. Signing the workThe finished app is signed with your own Apple developer certificate — your name, your account, your app.
  5. Delivery to TestFlightThe signed app uploads straight to Apple's TestFlight, ready to install on your testers' phones. About three minutes, tap to done.

What's real, and what's demo?

The demo on this site is a simulation — it shows the experience without touching any real accounts. The Builder itself is completely real: it's how our own game, Steamy Dumplings, gets built and shipped to TestFlight. Its first build, fired from a phone tap, landed in TestFlight on October 20, 2026.

Who it's for

Builders like us: people with more ideas than coding hours — indie makers, no-code creators, anyone whose workshop is an AI partner and a phone. If you've ever thought "I could make that app, if only I had a Mac," this factory door is the one we built for ourselves first.

What's next

Right now the Builder is a private tool in our own workshop. A version other builders can run for themselves — with their own accounts and their own keys — is on the workbench here at Hawkins Crafting Co. Watch this space, or follow along on YouTube, where we build in the open.
