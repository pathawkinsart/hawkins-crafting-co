      Setup & how sorting works

      Hold each card back so the WHOLE card fits inside the dashed frame — name and all — big, sharp and still — and tap the picture. The app reads the player's name on the card and looks it up in the loaded worksheet. It reads it right then, shows the user big and shouts it out loud — and the camera goes straight back to the live picture on its own, ready for the next card, with the last card's user still showing across the bottom of the picture and in the banner below it. Hold the next card up the same way and tap again. If a read comes back unpredictable, the fix is almost always the same: steadier, tap-and-hold on the name to focus, then tap. If you prefer hands-free reading, turn on the optional Live scan button — only then does the camera keep reading frame after frame until you turn it off.

      Every card you scan counts toward that user's total in the break: the first one calls out “1 of 6”, and what's left counts down — “5 to go”, “4 to go” — until the last card turns the banner green and calls out that all of that user's cards are found. If a user has more than one of the same player, each copy is its own row in the full list — “Copy 1 of 2”, “Copy 2 of 2” — and one scan checks off only one copy: that row gets a green ✓ CHECKED OFF in the list, the other copy stays “○ NOT YET” until you hold the next copy up and take its photo too — the camera is ready again on its own as soon as each card is called out. Scanning the same physical card twice never counts it twice, and once a card has been counted it is never called out again — if it slides back under, it shows on screen silently instead.

      How it reads the card: the app reads the whole card — the player's name printed on the front — and looks it up in the loaded worksheet, then sends it straight to its user. For Pokémon sets, the set number printed at the bottom-left also pins the exact card, so the app checks that too when it reads one. Commons and uncommons aren't named in the sheet — they go to the bulk piles for their break. It only asks you to tap when the card didn't read well enough to tell.
